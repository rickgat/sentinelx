## 1. Purpose and scope

This DPIA discharges the obligations under GDPR Article 35 and UU PDP Pasal 34 for processing activities that are likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. SentinelX performs offensive security testing, vulnerability scanning, breach-and-attack simulation (BAS), and continuous compliance attestation. Because we collect, process and route telemetry that may identify employees, customers, contractors and end users of our customer organisations, we must demonstrate that personal data processing is lawful, fair, transparent, minimised, accurate, time-limited, secure and accountable.

The DPIA covers:

- Customer-side personal data (operator identities, action audit logs, MFA factors).
- Subject-side personal data discovered during scans (usernames, email addresses, IP addresses, cookie identifiers, session tokens, leaked credentials).
- Telemetry and metadata generated by the SentinelX platform itself (operator session logs, billing identifiers, support tickets).
- Cross-border transfers between EU/EEA, UK, Indonesia, Singapore (primary infrastructure) and the United States (managed support).

Out of scope: anonymous aggregate metrics (k-anonymity > 50), public threat intelligence feeds.

### 8.2 Workflow stages

1. **Intake.** Request received via `dsr@sentinelx.example`, the in-product privacy centre, or postal mail. Ticket auto-created in DSR queue with SLA timer.
2. **Identity verification.** Two factor proof (email-of-record link + a piece of contextual knowledge or government ID matched against existing record). Refusal to verify within 14 days = closed-no-action with notification.
3. **Scope clarification.** If the request is ambiguous, the privacy team requests clarification within 5 business days; the SLA timer pauses (max 14 days).
4. **Search and assembly.** Run `dsr-search` job across tenant DB, audit log, support, billing, marketing, ATS. Output a per-system manifest.
5. **Controller routing (RPA-002, 003, 004).** Where SentinelX is processor, forward to controller within 7 days; close as forwarded.
6. **Decision.** Legal + DPO review for exemptions (e.g. ongoing investigation under Art. 23 GDPR / Pasal 15 UU PDP). Document the decision.
7. **Action.** Apply rectification, deletion, restriction or generate portable export. Erasure may be replaced by anonymisation where legally permitted.
8. **Notification.** Respond to data subject in the language of request with the action taken, basis for any refusal, and information on lodging a complaint.
9. **Audit.** Log to `dsr_audit` table; quarterly DPO review of SLA compliance.

### 8.3 Erasure decision tree

```
Request received
  -> Is requester verified?               No  -> Reject with reason
  -> Is data covered by legal retention?  Yes -> Restrict + notify; schedule purge at end of retention
  -> Is the request manifestly excessive? Yes -> Refuse, document
  -> Is SentinelX controller?             No  -> Forward to controller within 7 days
                                          Yes -> Execute erasure (see §8.3.1 for audit-chain)
  -> Cascading deletion required?         Yes -> Issue revocation to processors + backups (90-day grace)
  -> Confirm to data subject within SLA
```

### 8.3.1 Erasure interaction with the audit chain (P1-015)

The audit chain (`crates/sx-audit/src/chain.rs`) is a Merkle hash-chain
with RFC 3161 TSA anchors. **Deletion of a row breaks every subsequent
row's hash** and triggers a `category=BROKEN_CHAIN` CRITICAL incident.
Therefore, hard-delete is forbidden on `audit_events`. The Right to be
Forgotten is honoured through a defined "tombstone-and-redact" flow:

1. The DSR queue resolves the operator/subject to a stable `actor_id`.
2. `sx-cli audit redact` is invoked under the two-person rule. It:
   - Looks up every chain row where `actor.user_id = <subject_id>`.
   - Replaces the personally identifying columns (`actor.user.name`,
     `actor.user.email`, `actor.session.ip`, `request.headers.UA`)
     with `<redacted-2026-06-13-DSR-NNN>` placeholders.
   - **Preserves the row hash** by computing the redaction as a
     value-level overlay table (`audit_redactions`) — the chain
     itself stays byte-identical; reads JOIN against the overlay.
3. A new audit row is appended documenting the redaction itself
   (`action = privacy.dsr_redacted`) — this row is then chained and
   TSA-anchored at the next rotor.
4. The auditor verification key remains valid; the chain verifier
   exit code stays 0.

This is the only mechanism by which personal data is removed from the
audit chain. It satisfies Art. 17 GDPR / Pasal 8 UU PDP because (a) the
identifying fields are unrecoverable, (b) the redaction is itself
auditable, and (c) the chain integrity property is preserved so the
auditability claim used in §6 risk RPA-009 still holds.

WORM-side note: evidence bundles older than 7 days have already been
sealed under S3 Object Lock COMPLIANCE mode (see §10.1). The redaction
of operational rows therefore does not propagate into the bundle. The
bundle remains because it does not, by design, carry recoverable PII —
only aggregate counts and chain-row hashes.

### 8.3.2 DPO notification triggers

The DPO is notified within 4 hours when any of the following events
fire (`category=PRIVACY_NOTIFY` on the audit chain):

- A DSR comes in for a data class covered by a special-category basis
  under §4.1.
- A `privacy.dsr_redacted` audit row would touch more than 1000
  chain rows (suggests a high-value or long-tenure subject).
- The DLP detector matches a special-category class in flight (the
  request is rejected; the DPO is notified because the source path
  is producing such data).
- The Falco runtime emits a CRITICAL rule firing on a path that
  reads `iam.webauthn_credentials` or `device_trust.devices` from a
  process not on the allowlist.
- An evidence bundle export references a tenant with an open DSR.
- The audit chain verifier reports `BROKEN_CHAIN` for a row touching
  RPA-001 or RPA-011 data.

Notifications carry the `actor_id` of the subject (where known), the
RPA reference, the underlying audit row id, and a link to the DSR
ticket.

### 8.4 Portability format

CSV + JSON (UTF-8) bundle, signed with SentinelX release key (Ed25519), delivered via expiring HTTPS link (48 hours, single download) or encrypted email at the subject's choice.

### 10.1 Impact of 7-year WORM retention (P1-004) on personal data

The P1-004 evidence collector writes to an S3 bucket whose Object Lock
is configured in **COMPLIANCE mode** with a fixed 7-year retention; not
even the AWS root account can shorten or delete the objects during the
window. This has three consequences relevant to GDPR / UU PDP:

1. **No PII in the bundles.** Bundle payloads are *control evidence*,
   not subject data. Collectors emit aggregates (MFA enrolment rate,
   audit-chain verifier outcome, Vault sealed state, etc.). The
   one-off RPA-009 audit chain — which does carry operator identifiers
   — is anchored separately and its rows are immutable by hash, not by
   bucket policy.
2. **Erasure cannot reach the bundle.** Should an operator's row be
   erased from the operational store (§8.3 below), the bundle that
   already captured an aggregate including their action remains, but
   contains no recoverable identifier of that operator (the bundle
   stores only the count + the hash of the underlying audit row).
3. **Auditor verification stays offline.** Auditors verify bundles
   with the HMAC verifier key fetched at delivery time and run them
   on their own hardware; the WORM bucket is never directly read by
   an auditor.

If a single PII field is ever inadvertently written into an evidence
bundle (a defect class we treat as P0), the only remediation is to:

- Hash-anchor a redaction entry in the audit chain noting the
  affected object key.
- Notify the data subject and the supervisory authority within the
  applicable clock.
- Petition AWS Support for `LegalHold` review (which, per the SLA,
  can only adjust legal holds — not the retention itself).

That class of defect has never occurred in production. Mitigations
keeping it that way: schema-enforced collector outputs, snapshot
tests, the DLP inline-redactor in the bundle exporter path.

## 17. Breach notification specifics

Criteria:

- A breach is reportable to the supervisory authority if it is "likely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons" (GDPR Art. 33) / "berisiko terhadap pelanggaran hak Subjek Data Pribadi" (UU PDP Pasal 46).
- A breach is reportable to data subjects if the risk is "high".

Pre-determined templates: `templates/breach-notice-supervisory.md`, `templates/breach-notice-subject.md`. Both have variants in English and Bahasa Indonesia.

Indonesia-specific: within 3 x 24 hours of awareness; notice contains category, time, mitigation, contact, and is also published in summary form on the company website if required by the authority.

## 18. Children's data and vulnerable subjects

Although unintended, scans of customer-facing platforms may surface accounts that look like children's accounts. The operator runbook `runbooks/personal-data-handling.md` instructs:

1. Pause processing of that record.
2. Flag in DSR queue as `vulnerable-subject`.
3. Within 24h, anonymise or delete unless the customer (as controller) provides documented basis.

## 19. Open issues and known limitations

- Anonymous threat intel ingestion (RPA-010) periodically receives bulk dumps in which a single innocuous identifier could re-identify a subject when combined with other public data. We apply rolling deletion at 365 days and a takedown SLA of 10 business days.
- Indonesia adequacy decision from EU is still pending; until then we rely on SCC + supplementary measures (TIA).
- Support team uses Zendesk macros to redact; manual error remains the residual risk (MED). MIT-2026-01 addresses.

## Appendix A. Glossary

- **Controller** — the entity that determines the purposes and means of processing.
- **Processor** — the entity that processes on behalf of and on documented instructions of the controller.
- **Pseudonymisation** — processing such that data can no longer be attributed to a specific subject without additional information held separately.
- **Anonymisation** — irreversible removal of identifiability.
- **Special category data** — racial/ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, trade union membership, genetic/biometric data, health, sex life or orientation.

- **TIA** — Transfer Impact Assessment.
- **DPF** — EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

## Appendix C. DPIA methodology and stakeholder consultation

### C.1 Methodology

This DPIA follows the methodology recommended by the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) Guidelines on DPIA (WP248 rev.01) and the CNIL Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) framework, with localisation to UU PDP based on guidance issued by Kementerian Komunikasi dan Informatika. The seven steps applied are:

1. Identify the need for a DPIA (screening criteria below).
2. Describe the processing (sections 3 and 4).
3. Consult stakeholders (this appendix).
4. Assess necessity and proportionality (section 5).
5. Identify and assess risks (section 6).
6. Identify measures to mitigate risk (sections 7 and 10).
7. Sign off and record outcomes (section 20 + changelog).

### C.2 Screening criteria triggered

A DPIA is mandatory under GDPR Art. 35(3) and UU PDP Pasal 34 when one or more of the following apply. Items triggered for SentinelX are marked.

| Criterion | Triggered | Note |
|-----------|-----------|------|
| Systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects including profiling | No | Decision support only |
| Large-scale processing of special category data | No | Special category processing is incidental and minimised |
| Systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area | No | n/a |
| Innovative use of technology with privacy impact | Yes | LLM features and threat intel ingestion |
| Cross-border transfers to third countries without adequacy | Yes | EU -> SG, EU -> US, ID -> SG |
| Processing that prevents subjects from exercising a right | No | n/a |
| Use of biometric data | No | n/a |
| Vulnerable subjects | Partial | Audit log may incidentally capture data of vulnerable subjects |

### C.3 Stakeholders consulted

| Stakeholder | Consultation method | Date | Outcome |
|-------------|--------------------|------|---------|
| Internal DPO and Privacy Counsel | Two workshops | 2026-04-22, 2026-05-08 | Methodology and risk thresholds agreed |

### C.4 Residual risk acceptance

All residual risks at LOW and MED have been documented and accepted by the CISO and DPO. The acceptance is conditional on:

- Mitigation register milestones being met or re-baselined with executive notification.
- Quarterly delta reviews not surfacing new HIGH risks without supervisory consultation.
- Annual board-level review of effectiveness.

### C.5 Continuous improvement signals

The DPIA is reviewed when any of the following signals fire:

- A new processing activity is registered in the ROPA.
- A sub-processor change crosses a borderline (region, role, criticality).
- A regulatory enforcement decision is published that materially affects assumptions.
- A breach (notifiable or not) occurs.
- A material customer requests a copy of the DPIA and raises objections.
- The annual board review is due.
